## 1. What is This Chapter About?

**Ray Optics** is the study of how light behaves when it travels in straight lines (rays).

Think of everyday examples:
- Why do we see our **reflection in a mirror**? → **Reflection of light**
- Why does a **straw in water look bent**? → **Refraction of light**
- Why does a **diamond sparkle** so brilliantly? → **Total Internal Reflection**
- Why does a **prism split white light** into colours? → **Dispersion**
- How does a **microscope** make tiny things visible? → **Optical Instruments**
- Why does the **sky appear blue** and **sunset appear red**? → **Scattering of light**

This chapter answers all of these — it is the **most important chapter** for Bihar Board with a weightage of **14 marks** (highest of all chapters!).

**Topics covered:**
- Spherical mirrors — image formation, mirror formula
- Refraction, Snell's law, refractive index
- Total Internal Reflection and optical fibres
- Lenses — convex and concave, lens formula, lens maker's formula
- Prism — deviation, dispersion
- Optical instruments — eye, microscope, telescope

## 2. Reflection of Light by Spherical Mirrors

### 2.1 Important Terms and Definitions

```
                C        F        P
    ←——————————•————————•————————•
    (Centre    (Focus)  (Pole)
    of Curvature)

        R = radius of curvature
        f = focal length
        f = R/2
```

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Pole (P)** | The midpoint (centre) of the reflecting surface of a spherical mirror |
| **Centre of Curvature (C)** | The centre of the sphere of which the mirror is a part |
| **Radius of Curvature (R)** | Distance between pole P and centre of curvature C |
| **Principal Axis** | The straight line passing through P and C |
| **Principal Focus (F)** | The point on the axis where rays parallel to the axis converge (concave) or appear to diverge from (convex) after reflection |
| **Focal Length (f)** | Distance between pole P and focus F; **f = R/2** |
| **Aperture** | The diameter of the reflecting surface |

### 2.2 Relation between Focal Length and Radius of Curvature ⭐

```
f = R / 2
```

**Proof:** Consider a ray parallel to the principal axis hitting a concave mirror at point M. It reflects through F. The normal at M passes through C. Since the angle of incidence = angle of reflection, and using geometry (isoceles triangle MCF), we can show FP = PC/2, i.e., f = R/2.

### 2.3 Sign Convention (Cartesian)

- All distances are measured **from the pole P**.
- Distances in the direction of **incident light** → **positive** (right side).
- Distances **opposite** to incident light → **negative** (left side).
- **Heights above** the principal axis → **positive**.
- **Heights below** the principal axis → **negative**.

For a concave mirror (reflecting surface on the left):
- Object is placed in front → u is negative
- Focal length → f is negative (concave), f is positive (convex)
- Real images → v is negative; virtual images → v is positive

### 2.4 Image Formation by Concave Mirror

| Object Position | Image Position | Nature | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| At infinity | At F | Real, Inverted | Highly diminished (point) |
| Beyond C | Between F and C | Real, Inverted | Diminished |
| At C | At C | Real, Inverted | Same size |
| Between C and F | Beyond C | Real, Inverted | Magnified |
| At F | At infinity | Real, Inverted | Infinitely large |
| Between F and P | Behind mirror | Virtual, Erect | Magnified |

### 2.5 Image Formation by Convex Mirror

- Always forms **virtual, erect, and diminished** image.
- Image is always formed **behind the mirror** (between P and F).
- Used as **rear-view mirrors** in vehicles (gives wider field of view).

## 3. Mirror Formula and Magnification

### 3.1 Mirror Formula ⭐⭐ (Derivation — Board Exam 3-5 Marks)

**Statement:** The relationship between object distance (u), image distance (v), and focal length (f) of a spherical mirror is:

```
1/v + 1/u = 1/f
```

**Derivation (for concave mirror — real image):**

```
                          A
                          |
    ———————————C——————F———P———————
                          |
                          A'
```

Consider a concave mirror with pole P, focus F, and centre of curvature C.
Object AB is placed beyond C.

Draw two rays from A:
1. Ray parallel to principal axis → reflects through F
2. Ray through C → reflects back on same path

These two reflected rays meet at A' (image of A).

From the diagram, triangles ABP and A'B'P are similar:
```
A'B'/AB = PB'/PB        ...(1)
```

Also, triangles A'B'F and MPF are similar (M is where ray hits the mirror):
```
A'B'/MP = (PB' − PF)/PF
```

Since MP = AB (AB ≈ MP for small aperture/paraxial approximation):
```
A'B'/AB = (PB' − PF)/PF        ...(2)
```

From (1) and (2):
```
PB'/PB = (PB' − PF)/PF
PB' × PF = PB × (PB' − PF)
PB' × PF = PB × PB' − PB × PF
```

Dividing by PB × PB' × PF:
```
1/PB = 1/PF − 1/PB'
```

Applying sign convention: PB = −u, PB' = −v, PF = −f:
```
1/(−u) = 1/(−f) − 1/(−v)
−1/u = −1/f + 1/v
```

**Mirror Formula:**
```
1/v + 1/u = 1/f
```
✓

### 3.2 Magnification ⭐

**Linear (Transverse) Magnification:**

```
m = −v/u = h'/h
```

Where:
- `m` = magnification
- `v` = image distance
- `u` = object distance
- `h'` = height of image
- `h` = height of object

**Interpretation:**
- m is negative → image is **inverted** (real)
- m is positive → image is **erect** (virtual)
- |m| > 1 → image is **magnified**
- |m| < 1 → image is **diminished**
- |m| = 1 → image is **same size**

## 4. Refraction of Light — Laws and Refractive Index

### 4.1 Refraction of Light

**Definition:** *"The bending of light as it passes from one transparent medium to another is called refraction."*

**Cause:** Change in speed of light when it goes from one medium to another.

- Light slows down when going from rarer (air) to denser (glass) medium → bends **toward the normal**.
- Light speeds up when going from denser to rarer medium → bends **away from normal**.

### 4.2 Laws of Refraction (Snell's Law) ⭐⭐

**Law 1:** The incident ray, refracted ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the **same plane**.

**Law 2 (Snell's Law):** *"The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is a constant for a given pair of media."*

```
sin i / sin r = constant = n₂₁ = n₂/n₁
```

**Or:**
```
n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
```

Where:
- `i` = angle of incidence
- `r` = angle of refraction
- `n₁` = refractive index of first medium
- `n₂` = refractive index of second medium

### 4.3 Refractive Index ⭐

**Definition:** *"The refractive index of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to the speed of light in that medium."*

```
n = c / v
```

Where:
- `n` = refractive index (dimensionless)
- `c` = speed of light in vacuum = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
- `v` = speed of light in the medium

**Relative Refractive Index:**
```
n₂₁ = n₂/n₁ = v₁/v₂ = sin i / sin r
```

**Common values:**
| Medium | Refractive Index (n) |
|---|---|
| Vacuum/Air | 1.0 |
| Water | 1.33 |
| Glass | 1.5 |
| Diamond | 2.42 |

> 📝 **Key fact:** Higher refractive index = denser medium = light travels slower = bends more.

### 4.4 Relation between n, λ, and v

When light goes from one medium to another:
- **Frequency remains constant** (ν = constant)
- Speed changes: v = c/n
- Wavelength changes: λ_medium = λ_vacuum / n

```
n₁λ₁ = n₂λ₂     (since frequency is same)
```

## 5. Total Internal Reflection

### 5.1 Definition and Concept ⭐⭐ (Board Exam — 3 Marks)

**Definition:** *"When light travels from a denser medium to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, the light is completely reflected back into the denser medium. This phenomenon is called Total Internal Reflection (TIR)."*

**Conditions for TIR:**
1. Light must travel from a **denser medium to a rarer medium** (e.g., glass → air, water → air).
2. The angle of incidence must be **greater than the critical angle** (i > iC).

### 5.2 Critical Angle ⭐⭐

**Definition:** *"The critical angle is the angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the rarer medium is exactly 90°."*

**Formula:**
```
sin iC = n₂/n₁ = 1/n    (when second medium is air, n₂ = 1)
```

Or:
```
sin iC = 1/n
```

Where n is the refractive index of the denser medium with respect to air.

**Examples:**
- Glass (n = 1.5): sin iC = 1/1.5 = 0.667 → iC ≈ 42°
- Water (n = 1.33): sin iC = 1/1.33 → iC ≈ 49°
- Diamond (n = 2.42): sin iC = 1/2.42 → iC ≈ 24° (very small — why it sparkles!)

### 5.3 Applications of Total Internal Reflection ⭐⭐ (Board Exam Favourite)

**1. Optical Fibres:**
- Long, thin, flexible glass fibres
- Light enters at one end and undergoes repeated total internal reflections along the fibre
- Emerges at the other end with minimal loss
- Used in: **internet cables, telephone cables, medical endoscopy (to see inside body), decorative lamps**

```
   Light in → ////TIR////TIR////TIR//// → Light out
              (core: denser glass)
              (cladding: less dense glass)
```

**2. Mirage:**
- Hot air near the ground acts as a rarer medium.
- Light from the sky undergoes TIR in the hot air layers and appears to come from the ground.
- The viewer sees what appears to be a pool of water (reflection of the sky) on a hot road.

**3. Sparkling of Diamond:**
- Diamond has a very high refractive index (n = 2.42) → very small critical angle (~24°).
- Once light enters a diamond, it undergoes multiple total internal reflections before emerging.
- This causes the brilliance and sparkle.

**4. Prism in Periscopes and Binoculars:**
- Right-angle prisms use TIR (i = 45° > critical angle for glass ≈ 42°) to reflect light by 90° or 180°.
- More efficient than silvered mirrors (no energy loss in absorption).

**5. Looming (Superior Mirage):**
- Objects (like ships) appear to float above the horizon in cold regions due to TIR in cold air layers.

## 6. Refraction at Spherical Surfaces

### 6.1 Formula ⭐⭐ (Derivation — Board Exam)

For refraction at a single spherical surface separating two media with refractive indices n₁ and n₂, with centre of curvature C and radius R:

```
n₂/v − n₁/u = (n₂ − n₁)/R
```

Where:
- `u` = object distance (from the surface)
- `v` = image distance
- `R` = radius of curvature of the surface
- `n₁` = refractive index of the medium where the object is
- `n₂` = refractive index of the medium where the image forms

**Sign Convention:** Same as mirror — distances measured from the refracting surface.

**Derivation (Brief):**

Consider a convex surface. A ray from object O in medium n₁ refracts at point M and converges to image I in medium n₂.

Using small angle approximation (paraxial rays) and Snell's law at point M, and applying geometry with the angles:

```
n₁/u + n₂/v = (n₂ − n₁)/R     (with sign convention applied)
```

This gives the final result:
```
n₂/v − n₁/u = (n₂ − n₁)/R
```

## 7. Lenses — Thin Lens Formula

### 7.1 Types of Lenses

**Convex (Converging) Lens:** Thicker in the middle, converges light rays.

**Concave (Diverging) Lens:** Thinner in the middle, diverges light rays.

```
Convex Lens:        Concave Lens:
  |   |                | | |
  |   |               /   \
  |   |              |     |
  (thicker middle)   (thinner middle)
```

### 7.2 Important Terms

- **Optical Centre (O):** The central point of the lens through which rays pass without deviation.
- **Principal Axis:** Line through optical centre perpendicular to the lens.
- **Principal Focus (F):** For convex lens — real focus (converging point). For concave lens — virtual focus (diverging point).
- **Focal Length (f):** Distance from optical centre to principal focus.
  - Convex lens: **f is positive**
  - Concave lens: **f is negative**

### 7.3 Image Formation by Convex Lens

| Object Position | Image Position | Nature | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| At infinity | At F₂ | Real, Inverted | Highly diminished |
| Beyond 2F₁ | Between F₂ and 2F₂ | Real, Inverted | Diminished |
| At 2F₁ | At 2F₂ | Real, Inverted | Same size |
| Between F₁ and 2F₁ | Beyond 2F₂ | Real, Inverted | Magnified |
| At F₁ | At infinity | Real, Inverted | Infinitely large |
| Between F₁ and O | Same side as object | Virtual, Erect | Magnified |

### 7.4 Thin Lens Formula ⭐⭐

```
1/v − 1/u = 1/f
```

Where:
- `v` = image distance (from optical centre)
- `u` = object distance (from optical centre)
- `f` = focal length

**Sign Convention:** Distances measured from optical centre O.
- Object on left of lens: u is negative
- Convex lens: f is positive
- Concave lens: f is negative
- Real image (on right of convex lens): v is positive
- Virtual image (on left of lens): v is negative

### 7.5 Linear Magnification by Lens

```
m = v/u = h'/h
```

(Note: For lenses, m = v/u; for mirrors, m = −v/u — don't confuse!)

## 8. Lens Maker's Formula

### 8.1 Formula and Derivation ⭐⭐⭐ (5-Mark Board Question)

**The Lens Maker's Formula** relates the focal length of a lens to the refractive index and radii of curvature of its two surfaces.

```
1/f = (n − 1) [1/R₁ − 1/R₂]
```

Where:
- `f` = focal length of the lens
- `n` = refractive index of the lens material (with respect to surrounding medium, usually air)
- `R₁` = radius of curvature of the first surface (surface the light hits first)
- `R₂` = radius of curvature of the second surface

**Sign Convention for R:**
- Centre of curvature on the right side of surface → R is **positive**
- Centre of curvature on the left side → R is **negative**

**Derivation:**

A thin lens has two refracting surfaces with radii R₁ and R₂. Let the lens material have refractive index n, surrounded by air (n₁ = 1).

**Step 1:** Refraction at the first surface:
```
n/v₁ − 1/u = (n − 1)/R₁        ...(i)
```
(Image formed at v₁ acts as the object for the second surface)

**Step 2:** Refraction at the second surface (the image from first surface is the object):
```
1/v − n/v₁ = (1 − n)/R₂        ...(ii)
```

**Step 3:** Add equations (i) and (ii):
```
1/v − 1/u = (n − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂)
```

Since 1/v − 1/u = 1/f (thin lens formula):

**Lens Maker's Formula:**
```
1/f = (n − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂)
```
✓

**For a biconvex lens:** R₁ > 0, R₂ < 0 → 1/f is positive → f is positive (converging lens) ✓

## 9. Power of a Lens

### 9.1 Definition and Formula ⭐⭐

**Definition:** *"The power of a lens is defined as the reciprocal of its focal length in metres."*

```
P = 1/f
```

Where:
- `P` = Power of lens
- `f` = focal length in **metres**

**SI Unit:** **Dioptre (D)**

```
1 Dioptre = 1 m⁻¹  (power of a lens with focal length 1 metre)
```

**Sign:**
- Convex (converging) lens: f is positive → P is **positive**
- Concave (diverging) lens: f is negative → P is **negative**

**Example:**
- A convex lens with f = 25 cm = 0.25 m → P = 1/0.25 = **+4 D**
- A concave lens with f = −50 cm = −0.5 m → P = 1/(−0.5) = **−2 D**

> 📝 **Board Exam:** "A person uses spectacles of power −2.5 D. What type of defect does he have?" → Since P is negative, the lens is concave → The person is **myopic (short-sighted)**.

## 10. Combination of Lenses and Mirrors

### 10.1 Combination of Lenses in Contact ⭐

When two thin lenses are placed in **contact** (touching each other), the combination behaves as a single lens with:

**Equivalent focal length:**
```
1/F = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂
```

**Equivalent power:**
```
P = P₁ + P₂
```

Where f₁, f₂ are individual focal lengths and P₁, P₂ are individual powers.

**For n lenses in contact:**
```
1/F = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂ + 1/f₃ + ... + 1/fₙ
P = P₁ + P₂ + P₃ + ... + Pₙ
```

**Example:** A convex lens (f₁ = +30 cm) and a concave lens (f₂ = −10 cm) are in contact.
```
1/F = 1/30 + 1/(−10) = 1/30 − 3/30 = −2/30
F = −15 cm (concave — net diverging)
```

### 10.2 Silvered Lens (Lens + Mirror combination)

When a lens has one surface silvered, it acts as both a lens and a mirror. The effective power:
```
P_eff = P_lens + P_mirror + P_lens = 2P_lens + P_mirror
```

(Light refracts through the lens twice and reflects from the mirror once)

## 11. Refraction Through a Prism

### 11.1 Geometry of a Prism ⭐⭐

```
                A (apex angle)
               /\
              /  \
             /    \
            /      \
           /        \
          /__________\
         B            C
```

- **A** = Apex angle (angle of prism)
- **i** = angle of incidence
- **e** = angle of emergence
- **r₁** = angle of refraction at first surface
- **r₂** = angle of incidence at second surface
- **δ** = angle of deviation

### 11.2 Important Relations ⭐⭐

From geometry of the prism:

**Relation 1:**
```
r₁ + r₂ = A
```

**Relation 2:**
```
δ = (i + e) − A
```

**Or:**
```
i + e = A + δ
```

### 11.3 Angle of Minimum Deviation ⭐⭐⭐ (Most Important Derivation)

**Minimum Deviation (δm):** The angle of deviation is minimum when the ray passes **symmetrically** through the prism.

At minimum deviation:
- `i = e` (angles of incidence and emergence are equal)
- `r₁ = r₂ = r = A/2` (refraction is symmetric)

From the relations:
```
At δ = δm:    i + i = A + δm   →   2i = A + δm   →   i = (A + δm)/2
              r + r = A        →   2r = A          →   r = A/2
```

Applying Snell's Law at the first surface:
```
n = sin i / sin r = sin[(A + δm)/2] / sin(A/2)
```

**Formula for Refractive Index of Prism:**
```
         sin[(A + δm)/2]
n =    ——————————————————
            sin(A/2)
```

> 📝 **This is one of the most frequently asked 5-mark derivations in Bihar Board. Learn it step by step.**

## 12. Dispersion by a Prism

### 12.1 What is Dispersion? ⭐

**Definition:** *"The splitting of white light into its constituent colours (spectrum) when it passes through a prism is called dispersion."*

**Cause:** Different colours of light have different wavelengths → different refractive indices in glass → they bend by different amounts.

```
White light → PRISM → Violet (most bent)
                       Indigo
                       Blue
                       Green
                       Yellow
                       Orange
                       Red (least bent)
```

- **Violet** has the **shortest wavelength** → bends the most (highest n)
- **Red** has the **longest wavelength** → bends the least (lowest n)

### 12.2 Angular Dispersion

```
Angular dispersion = δv − δr
```

Where δv = deviation of violet, δr = deviation of red.

### 12.3 Dispersive Power

```
ω = (nv − nr) / (n_mean − 1) ≈ (δv − δr) / δ_mean
```

### 12.4 Cauchy's Formula (Qualitative Only)

The refractive index n depends on wavelength λ:
```
n = A + B/λ²
```

Where A and B are constants. As λ decreases (violet), n increases.

## 13. Some Natural Phenomena Due to Sunlight

### 13.1 The Rainbow ⭐ (2-Mark Theory)

A rainbow is formed due to **dispersion and total internal reflection** of sunlight in water droplets.

**Formation:**
1. Sunlight enters a water droplet and is refracted (dispersed into colours).
2. It undergoes **total internal reflection** at the back of the droplet.
3. It refracts again as it exits the droplet.
4. Different colours emerge at different angles.

- **Primary Rainbow:** One internal reflection; red on outside (42°), violet on inside (40°).
- **Secondary Rainbow:** Two internal reflections; order is reversed — violet outside, red inside. Fainter than primary.

### 13.2 Scattering of Light — Blue Sky and Red Sunset ⭐⭐

**Rayleigh's Law of Scattering:**
```
I ∝ 1/λ⁴
```

The intensity of scattered light is inversely proportional to the **fourth power** of the wavelength.

**Blue Sky:**
- When sunlight passes through the atmosphere, it is scattered by air molecules.
- Short wavelengths (violet, blue) are scattered much more than long wavelengths (red, orange).
- We see the sky as **blue** because scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all directions.

**Red Sunset (and Red Sunrise):**
- At sunset/sunrise, sunlight travels a **longer path** through the atmosphere.
- Most of the blue light is scattered away before reaching the observer.
- Only long wavelengths (red, orange) remain — so the Sun appears red.

**Why is the Sun white at noon?**
- At noon, sunlight travels the shortest path through the atmosphere.
- All wavelengths reach us nearly equally → Sun appears white (mix of all colours).

> 📝 **Board Exam:** "Why does the sky appear blue?" and "Why does the sun appear red at sunset?" are very common 2-mark questions.

## 14. Optical Instruments — The Human Eye

### 14.1 Structure of the Eye ⭐

```
   Cornea → Aqueous Humour → Iris/Pupil → Lens → Vitreous Humour → Retina → Optic Nerve
```

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Cornea** | Transparent layer at front; does most of the refraction of light |
| **Iris** | Coloured ring that controls the size of the pupil (controls light entering) |
| **Pupil** | The opening in the iris; dilates in dim light, contracts in bright light |
| **Crystalline Lens** | Adjusts its focal length (accommodation) to focus objects at various distances |
| **Ciliary Muscles** | Control the shape (curvature) of the lens |
| **Retina** | Screen at the back of the eye — contains light-sensitive cells (rods and cones) |
| **Optic Nerve** | Carries signals from retina to the brain |
| **Yellow Spot** | Most sensitive part of the retina; sharpest vision |
| **Blind Spot** | Where optic nerve leaves the retina; no photo-receptors; no image formed here |

### 14.2 Power of Accommodation

The ability of the eye lens to change its focal length to focus objects at various distances is called **accommodation**.

- **Near point (D):** Closest point the eye can see clearly = **25 cm** (for normal eye) — also called **Least Distance of Distinct Vision (LDDV)**
- **Far point:** The farthest point the eye can see clearly = **infinity** (for normal eye)

### 14.3 Defects of Vision and Correction ⭐⭐

**1. Myopia (Short-sightedness / Near-sightedness):**
- Can see nearby objects clearly but **cannot see distant objects**.
- **Cause:** Eyeball is too long OR lens is too curved → image forms in front of retina.
- **Correction:** **Concave lens** (diverging) of appropriate power.

**2. Hypermetropia (Long-sightedness / Far-sightedness):**
- Can see distant objects clearly but **cannot see nearby objects**.
- **Cause:** Eyeball is too short OR lens is not curved enough → image forms behind retina.
- **Correction:** **Convex lens** (converging) of appropriate power.

**3. Presbyopia:**
- Loss of accommodation with age (ciliary muscles weaken).
- Cannot see both near and far clearly.
- **Correction:** **Bifocal lenses** (convex for near, concave for far).

**4. Astigmatism:**
- Unequal curvature of the cornea → different focusing in different planes.
- **Correction:** **Cylindrical lenses**.

## 15. Simple Microscope (Magnifying Glass)

### 15.1 Construction and Working ⭐

A simple microscope is just a **single convex lens** of short focal length.

The object is placed between the lens and its focus (i.e., u < f).

The eye placed close to the lens sees a magnified, erect, virtual image.

### 15.2 Magnifying Power ⭐⭐

**Case 1: Image formed at near point (D = 25 cm)** ← Maximum magnification

```
m = 1 + D/f
```

**Case 2: Image formed at infinity** ← Comfortable viewing (relaxed eye)

```
m = D/f
```

Where:
- `D` = Least distance of distinct vision = 25 cm (for normal eye)
- `f` = focal length of the lens

**Example:** f = 5 cm
- Image at near point: m = 1 + 25/5 = **6**
- Image at infinity: m = 25/5 = **5**

## 16. Compound Microscope

### 16.1 Construction ⭐⭐

A compound microscope uses **two converging lenses**:

```
Object → [Objective Lens] → [Eyepiece (Ocular)] → Eye
          (short f, small)    (longer f)
```

- **Objective Lens:** Short focal length (f_o), small aperture. Placed close to the object.
- **Eyepiece:** Longer focal length (f_e). Acts like a simple microscope to further magnify the image from the objective.

**Working:**
1. The object is placed just beyond the focal length of the objective.
2. The objective forms a **real, magnified, inverted image** (called intermediate image) inside the tube, just within the focal length of the eyepiece.
3. The eyepiece then forms a further magnified **virtual image** at the near point or at infinity.

### 16.2 Magnifying Power of Compound Microscope ⭐⭐⭐

**Total magnification = (magnification by objective) × (magnification by eyepiece)**

```
m = m_o × m_e
```

Where:
- `m_o` = magnification by objective = L/f_o (approximately)
  - L = length of tube (distance between back focus of objective and front focus of eyepiece) = **tube length**
- `m_e` = magnification by eyepiece

**When final image is at near point (D):**
```
m = (L/f_o) × (1 + D/f_e)
```

**When final image is at infinity (relaxed eye):**
```
m = (L/f_o) × (D/f_e)
```

Where:
- `L` = image distance from objective (≈ tube length)
- `f_o` = focal length of objective
- `f_e` = focal length of eyepiece
- `D` = 25 cm

> 📝 **Board Exam Note:** The formula **m = (L/f_o)(D/f_e)** is the most commonly used form. Also, to increase magnification: use lenses with **smaller focal lengths** and increase the **tube length L**.

## 17. Telescope

### 17.1 Types of Telescopes

| Type | Uses | Mirrors/Lenses |
|---|---|---|
| Refracting (Astronomical) | Viewing stars/planets | Two convex lenses |
| Reflecting | Large observatories | Concave mirror + eyepiece |

### 17.2 Refracting Astronomical Telescope ⭐⭐⭐ (Most Important for Board)

**Construction:**
```
[Objective Lens]          [Eyepiece]
(large aperture,          (small aperture,
long focal length f_o)     short focal length f_e)
```

- The objective has a **large aperture** (to gather more light) and **large focal length**.
- The eyepiece has a **small focal length** (to give high magnification).
- The distance between the lenses = f_o + f_e (when object at infinity, final image also at infinity).

**Working (for object at infinity):**
1. Parallel rays from a distant object enter the objective lens.
2. The objective forms a **real, diminished, inverted image** at its focal plane (which coincides with the focal point of the eyepiece).
3. The eyepiece acts as a simple magnifier and forms the final **virtual image at infinity** (for relaxed eye viewing).

### 17.3 Magnifying Power of Telescope ⭐⭐⭐

**When final image is at infinity (normal adjustment):**
```
m = −f_o / f_e
```

The negative sign indicates the final image is **inverted**.

**When final image is at near point D:**
```
m = −(f_o/f_e) × (1 + f_e/D)
```

**Length of telescope (normal adjustment):**
```
L = f_o + f_e
```

> 📝 **Board Exam Tip:** "To increase the magnifying power of a telescope, what should be done?"
> → **Increase f_o (focal length of objective) OR decrease f_e (focal length of eyepiece).**

### 17.4 Reflecting Telescope (Brief) ⭐

- Uses a **large concave mirror** as the objective (instead of a lens).
- **Advantages over refracting telescope:**
  1. No chromatic aberration (mirrors reflect all wavelengths equally).
  2. Large mirrors are easier to make than large lenses (mirrors only need one polished surface).
  3. Can be made much larger → gather more light → see fainter objects.
  4. No sagging issue (mirrors can be supported from behind).

**Example:** The Hubble Space Telescope uses a reflecting design.

### 17.5 Terrestrial vs Astronomical Telescope

| Feature | Astronomical | Terrestrial |
|---|---|---|
| Final Image | Inverted | Erect |
| Extra element | None | Erecting lens or Galilean eyepiece |
| Length | Shorter | Longer |
| Use | Stars, planets | Objects on land |

## 19. Board Exam Questions with Answers

### Part A — 2-Mark Questions

---

**Q1. State the laws of reflection of light.**

**Answer:**
1. The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the **same plane**.
2. The angle of incidence (∠i) is always **equal** to the angle of reflection (∠r). i.e., ∠i = ∠r.

---

**Q2. State Snell's Law of refraction.**

**Answer:**
Snell's Law states: *"The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media."*

```
sin i / sin r = n₂₁ = constant
```

Or in the form: n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r

---

**Q3. Define Total Internal Reflection. What are its two conditions?**

**Answer:**
Total Internal Reflection is the phenomenon in which light traveling from a denser to a rarer medium is completely reflected back into the denser medium, with no refraction occurring.

**Two conditions:**
1. Light must travel from a **denser to a rarer medium**.
2. The angle of incidence must be **greater than the critical angle** (i > iC).

---

**Q4. Define the power of a lens. What is its SI unit?**

**Answer:**
The power of a lens is defined as the reciprocal of its focal length in metres:
```
P = 1/f (f in metres)
```

SI unit is **Dioptre (D)**. A converging lens has positive power; a diverging lens has negative power.

---

**Q5. Why does the sky appear blue?**

**Answer:**
Sunlight is scattered by air molecules in the atmosphere. According to Rayleigh's law, the intensity of scattered light is proportional to 1/λ⁴. Since blue light has a shorter wavelength than red light, it is scattered much more strongly in all directions. This scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all parts of the sky, making the sky appear blue.

---

**Q6. What is the least distance of distinct vision for a normal human eye? What is the far point?**

**Answer:**
- Least distance of distinct vision (near point) = **25 cm** (D = 25 cm)
- Far point of a normal eye = **infinity (∞)**

---

### Part B — 3-Mark Questions

---

**Q7. What is Total Internal Reflection? Give two applications.**

**Answer:**
Total Internal Reflection (TIR) occurs when light travels from a denser to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, causing complete reflection back into the denser medium.

**Application 1 — Optical Fibres:**
In optical fibres, light undergoes repeated total internal reflections along the length of the fibre (glass core surrounded by less dense cladding). Used in internet cables, telephone communication, and medical endoscopy.

**Application 2 — Sparkling of Diamond:**
Diamond has a very high refractive index (n = 2.42), giving a very small critical angle (~24°). Light entering a diamond undergoes multiple total internal reflections before exiting, causing it to sparkle and shine brilliantly.

---

**Q8. Explain with diagram the formation of a rainbow.**

**Answer:**
A rainbow is formed due to the **dispersion** and **total internal reflection** of sunlight in water droplets suspended in the atmosphere after rain.

**Formation process:**
1. Sunlight enters a spherical water droplet and is **refracted** (dispersed into colours) at the entry surface.
2. The dispersed light undergoes **total internal reflection** at the back surface of the droplet.
3. On exiting the droplet, the light is **refracted** again, further separating the colours.
4. Violet light (shorter wavelength) exits at about 40° and red light (longer wavelength) at about 42° with respect to the incoming sunlight direction.

**Result:** An observer sees red on the outer arc and violet on the inner arc of the primary rainbow.

```
Sun's rays →   [Water Droplet]
               Enter → Disperse → TIR → Exit & refract
               
Observer sees:  Red (outer, 42°)
               ...colours...
               Violet (inner, 40°)
```

---

**Q9. What is the magnifying power of a simple microscope? Derive the expression.**

**Answer:**
The magnifying power (m) of a simple microscope (convex lens of focal length f) is the ratio of the angle subtended by the image at the eye to the angle subtended by the object when placed at the near point D.

**When the image forms at the near point D:**

```
m = 1 + D/f
```

**When the image forms at infinity (relaxed eye):**
```
m = D/f
```

**Derivation (image at D):**

When the object is at the near point D without the lens, the angle subtended:
```
α₀ = h/D     (where h = height of object)
```

With the lens, the virtual image at D has height h' and the angle:
```
α = h'/D
```

But by the definition of magnification by lens:
```
m = h'/h = 1 + D/f
```

(This follows from the lens formula: when image is at −D, object at u gives 1/v − 1/u = 1/f → 1/u = 1/v − 1/f → using v = −D: m = 1 + D/f)

**Q10. Derive the mirror formula 1/v + 1/u = 1/f for a concave mirror.**

**Answer (Full Derivation):**

Consider a concave mirror with pole P, focus F, and centre of curvature C.
Let the focal length PF = f and radius PC = R = 2f.
An object AB is placed beyond C. Let its image A'B' form between F and C.

**Ray diagram:**
- Ray 1: Parallel to principal axis → reflects through F.
- Ray 2: Through C → reflects back on same path.
- The two reflected rays meet at A'.

**Derivation using similar triangles:**

Triangle ABP and Triangle A'B'P are similar:
```
A'B'/AB = PB'/PB = v/u    (using magnitudes for now)
```

Triangle A'B'F and Triangle MPF are similar (M is point on mirror, MP ≈ AB for small aperture):
```
A'B'/AB = (PB' − PF)/PF = (v − f)/f
```

Equating both expressions:
```
v/u = (v − f)/f
vf = u(v − f)
vf = uv − uf
```

Dividing throughout by uvf:
```
1/u = 1/f − 1/v
```

**∴ 1/v + 1/u = 1/f** ✓

This is the mirror formula (with sign convention: f is negative for concave mirror, u is negative for real object).

### Part D — Numericals

---

**Q14. A concave mirror has a focal length of 15 cm. Where should an object be placed so that its image is formed at 60 cm in front of the mirror?**

**Solution:**
Given: f = −15 cm (concave), v = −60 cm (real image in front of mirror)

Using mirror formula:
```
1/v + 1/u = 1/f
1/(−60) + 1/u = 1/(−15)
1/u = −1/15 + 1/60 = −4/60 + 1/60 = −3/60 = −1/20
u = −20 cm
```

**The object should be placed 20 cm in front of the mirror.**

---

**Q15. A convex lens has a focal length of 20 cm. Find the image position when an object is placed 30 cm from the lens. Also find the magnification.**

**Solution:**
Given: f = +20 cm, u = −30 cm

Thin lens formula:
```
1/v − 1/u = 1/f
1/v − 1/(−30) = 1/20
1/v + 1/30 = 1/20
1/v = 1/20 − 1/30 = 3/60 − 2/60 = 1/60
v = +60 cm
```

Magnification:
```
m = v/u = 60/(−30) = −2
```

**The image is formed 60 cm on the other side of the lens. Magnification = −2 (real, inverted, magnified).**

---

**Q16. A glass prism (n = 1.5) has an apex angle of 60°. Find the angle of minimum deviation.**

**Solution:**
Given: n = 1.5, A = 60°

```
n = sin[(A + δm)/2] / sin(A/2)
1.5 = sin[(60° + δm)/2] / sin(30°)
1.5 = sin[(60° + δm)/2] / 0.5
sin[(60° + δm)/2] = 1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75
(60° + δm)/2 = sin⁻¹(0.75) = 48.59°
60° + δm = 97.18°
δm = 37.18° ≈ 37°
```

**Angle of minimum deviation ≈ 37°**

---

**Q17. A compound microscope has an objective of focal length 2 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 5 cm. The tube length is 15 cm. Find the total magnification (image at infinity).**

**Solution:**
Given: f_o = 2 cm, f_e = 5 cm, L = 15 cm, D = 25 cm

```
m = (L/f_o) × (D/f_e)
m = (15/2) × (25/5)
m = 7.5 × 5 = 37.5
```

**Total magnification = 37.5**

---

**Q18. A telescope has an objective of focal length 100 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 5 cm. Find (a) magnifying power (b) length of telescope.**

**Solution:**
Given: f_o = 100 cm, f_e = 5 cm

(a) Magnifying power (normal adjustment):
```
m = f_o/f_e = 100/5 = 20
```

(b) Length of telescope:
```
L = f_o + f_e = 100 + 5 = 105 cm
```

**Magnifying power = 20; Length = 105 cm**

---

**Q19. The refractive index of glass is 1.5. Find the critical angle for glass-air interface.**

**Solution:**
```
sin iC = 1/n = 1/1.5 = 2/3 = 0.6667
iC = sin⁻¹(0.6667) ≈ 41.8° ≈ 42°
```

**Critical angle for glass-air ≈ 42°**

```
│
└── OPTICAL INSTRUMENTS
      ├── Human Eye → Accommodation, near point (25 cm), Myopia, Hypermetropia
      ├── Simple Microscope → m = D/f or 1 + D/f
      ├── Compound Microscope → m = (L/f_o)(D/f_e)
      └── Telescope → m = f_o/f_e, L = f_o + f_e
```
