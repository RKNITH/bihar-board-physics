## 1. What is This Chapter About?

This chapter is about the **structure of the atom** — what's inside it, how electrons move, and why atoms emit coloured light.

Think of it this way:
- Why do neon signs glow in different colours? → Because **atoms emit specific wavelengths of light**.
- How do scientists know that atoms have a tiny nucleus? → Because of **Rutherford's famous scattering experiment**.
- Why don't electrons fall into the nucleus? → **Bohr's model** gives the answer.

**What you will learn:**
- Thomson's and Rutherford's atomic models — and their failures
- Alpha-particle scattering experiment — the most important experiment in atomic physics
- Rutherford's nuclear model
- Bohr's postulates and how he explained hydrogen's spectrum
- Spectral series of hydrogen (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen, etc.)
- De Broglie's explanation of Bohr's second postulate

## 2. Early Atomic Models

### 2.1 Thomson's Model (Plum Pudding Model) — 1904

**Description:**
- J.J. Thomson proposed this model after discovering the electron in 1897.
- The atom is a **sphere of positive charge** (like a pudding) with **electrons embedded in it** (like plums).
- The positive charge is uniformly distributed throughout the sphere.
- The electrons are stuck inside this positive cloud.

```
    ___________
   /  + + + + +\
  |  + ⊖ + ⊖ + |
  |  + + ⊖ + + |    ⊖ = electron
  |  + ⊖ + + + |    +  = positive charge (uniform)
   \___________/
  Thomson's Plum Pudding Model
```

**Why it Failed:**
- It could NOT explain Rutherford's alpha-particle scattering results.
- It predicted that alpha particles would pass straight through or deflect at very small angles — but in the experiment, some particles bounced BACK.

## 3. Alpha Particle Scattering Experiment

### 3.1 Experimental Setup ⭐⭐⭐ (Most Important — Board Exam Must!)

**Performed by:** Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden, under the supervision of **Ernest Rutherford** (1911)

**Setup:**
- A radioactive source (Radium) emits **alpha (α) particles**.
- These alpha particles are directed at a very thin **gold foil** (about 1000 atoms thick).
- A circular **zinc sulphide (ZnS) screen** surrounds the foil. It glows (scintillates) wherever an alpha particle hits.
- The whole apparatus is in a **vacuum** to prevent absorption of alpha particles by air.

```
         [Radioactive Source]
               ↓
         [Lead Collimator] ← makes a narrow beam
               ↓
         [Thin Gold Foil]
          ↙    ↓    ↘
   deflected  straight  bounced
    (small   through    back
    angle)             (rare)
          
   [Circular ZnS Screen around foil]
```

**Alpha particle (α):**
- Nucleus of Helium atom: 2 protons + 2 neutrons
- Charge = +2e
- Mass = 4 u (atomic mass units)
- Fast moving (speed ≈ 2×10⁷ m/s)

### 3.2 Observations ⭐⭐ (Very Important for Board)

| Observation | What was Seen |
|---|---|
| **1** | Most alpha particles passed **straight through** the gold foil without any deflection. |
| **2** | Some alpha particles were deflected by **small angles** (less than a few degrees). |
| **3** | A very **small number** (about 1 in 8000) were deflected at **large angles** (more than 90°). |
| **4** | A very **few** (about 1 in 20,000) bounced **straight back** (deflected by ~180°). |

> 📝 **Rutherford's famous quote:** *"It was quite the most incredible event that has ever happened to me in my life. It was almost as incredible as if you fired a 15-inch shell at a piece of tissue paper and it came back and hit you."*

### 3.3 Conclusions from the Experiment ⭐⭐

From each observation, Rutherford drew specific conclusions:

| Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Most particles pass straight through | Atom is mostly **empty space**. |
| Small deflections of some particles | There is a positive charge inside the atom (like charges repel alpha particles). |
| Large angle deflections | The positive charge is concentrated in a very **small, dense region** — the nucleus. |
| Particles bouncing back | The nucleus is extremely **hard and dense** — direct head-on collision causes reversal. |

### 3.4 Distance of Closest Approach ⭐ (Numerical Important)

When an alpha particle moves **directly toward** the nucleus, it slows down due to electrostatic repulsion, stops momentarily, then bounces back.

At the point of closest approach, all kinetic energy of alpha particle converts to potential energy:

**Kinetic Energy = Electrostatic Potential Energy**

```
½ mv² = (1/4πε₀) × (2e)(Ze) / r₀
```

Where:
- `m` = mass of alpha particle
- `v` = initial velocity of alpha particle
- `Ze` = charge on nucleus (Z = atomic number)
- `2e` = charge on alpha particle
- `r₀` = distance of closest approach

**Solving for r₀:**
```
         1     2Ze²
r₀ = ————————  ———————
       4πε₀    (½mv²)
```

Or:
```
         2kZe²
r₀ = ——————————
          E_k
```
Where `E_k` = kinetic energy of alpha particle.

**Physical significance:** r₀ gives an estimate of the **size of the nucleus** (order of 10⁻¹⁵ m = 1 femtometre = 1 fm).

### 3.5 Impact Parameter (b) ⭐

**Definition:** The **perpendicular distance** between the initial path of the alpha particle and the centre of the nucleus is called the **impact parameter (b)**.

```
              | b (impact parameter)
              |
→ α particle →|————————— Nucleus
              |
```

**Relation between b and scattering angle (θ):**
```
         Ze² cot(θ/2)
b = ————————————————————
         4πε₀ × E_k
```

Where E_k = kinetic energy of alpha particle.

**Key relationship:**
- **b = 0** (head-on): θ = 180° (particle bounces straight back)
- **b is small**: θ is large (large deflection)
- **b is large**: θ is small (small deflection)
- **b >> nuclear size**: θ ≈ 0° (particle goes straight through)

> 📝 **Board Exam Note:** "Smaller the impact parameter, larger the scattering angle." — This is a 1-mark statement asked in many years.

## 4. Rutherford's Nuclear Model of Atom

### 4.1 Postulates of Rutherford's Model ⭐⭐

Based on his scattering experiment, Rutherford proposed:

1. **The atom has a tiny, dense, positively charged centre called the nucleus.**
   - Almost all the mass of the atom is concentrated in the nucleus.
   - The nucleus is extremely small (radius ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ m) compared to the atom (radius ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m).

2. **The nucleus is surrounded by electrons that revolve around it in circular orbits.**
   - Just like planets revolve around the sun (hence called the "Solar System Model" or "Planetary Model").

3. **The electrostatic force of attraction between the nucleus and electrons provides the centripetal force** for circular motion.

4. **The atom is electrically neutral** — total negative charge of electrons = positive charge of nucleus.

5. **The atom is mostly empty space.** The size of nucleus is about 10⁻⁵ times the size of the atom.

```
          e⁻ → orbit
         /
        e⁻                    Scale comparison:
        |   nucleus (+Ze)      Nucleus : Atom
        e⁻                    = 1 cm : 1 km (roughly!)
         \
          e⁻
```

### 4.2 Drawbacks of Rutherford's Model ⭐⭐ (Board Exam — 2 or 3 marks)

**Drawback 1: Could not explain atomic stability**
- According to classical electromagnetic theory, an **accelerating charge must radiate energy**.
- An electron in circular orbit is continuously accelerating (centripetal acceleration).
- So it should continuously emit energy → lose energy → spiral inward → fall into nucleus.
- The atom should collapse in about **10⁻⁸ seconds**. But atoms are stable — this is a contradiction!

**Drawback 2: Could not explain the line spectrum of hydrogen**
- If an electron continuously radiates while spiralling inward, it should emit **continuous spectrum** of all frequencies.
- But experimentally, hydrogen emits only **specific discrete wavelengths** (line spectrum).
- Rutherford's model gives no explanation for this.

> 📝 **Key Point to remember:** Rutherford's model fails because of **classical electromagnetic theory** — any accelerating charge must radiate energy. Circular motion = continuous acceleration = continuous energy loss = atom should collapse.

## 5. Atomic Spectra

### 5.1 What is a Spectrum?

When white light passes through a **prism**, it splits into different colours (rainbow). This is called a **continuous spectrum** — all wavelengths are present.

When light from heated gases (like hydrogen) passes through a prism, we get only certain specific bright coloured lines. This is called a **line spectrum** or **emission spectrum**.

### 5.2 Emission Spectrum vs Absorption Spectrum

| Emission Spectrum | Absorption Spectrum |
|---|---|
| Bright coloured lines on dark background | Dark lines on continuous coloured background |
| When atoms emit light | When atoms absorb light |
| Gas at high temperature emits light | Cool gas absorbs specific wavelengths from white light |

### 5.3 Hydrogen Spectrum — Balmer's Formula ⭐⭐

In 1885, **J.J. Balmer** found a formula that fitted the visible lines of hydrogen spectrum:

```
1/λ = R_H (1/2² − 1/n²)      where n = 3, 4, 5, 6, ...
```

Where:
- `λ` = wavelength of emitted light
- `R_H` = Rydberg constant = **1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹**
- `n` = 3, 4, 5, 6, ... (upper energy level)

**General formula (Rydberg formula):**
```
1/λ = R_H (1/n₁² − 1/n₂²)
```
Where n₁ < n₂, both are positive integers.

This formula works for ALL series of hydrogen spectrum (not just visible lines).

## 6. Bohr's Model of Hydrogen Atom

### 6.1 Introduction

In 1913, **Niels Bohr** proposed a new model that solved the problems of Rutherford's model. He combined classical mechanics with Planck's quantum idea.

### 6.2 Bohr's Postulates ⭐⭐⭐ (Most Important — 3 or 5 Mark)

**Postulate 1: Quantization of Orbits (Stationary Orbits)**

*"Electrons revolve around the nucleus only in certain allowed circular orbits (called stationary orbits or permitted orbits). While revolving in these orbits, the electron does NOT emit radiation."*

These orbits are called **stationary states**. The electron has a fixed energy in each orbit.

**Postulate 2: Quantization of Angular Momentum**

*"The angular momentum of the electron in a stationary orbit is an integral multiple of h/2π (ħ)."*

```
        nh
mvr = ——————
       2π
```

Where:
- `m` = mass of electron
- `v` = velocity of electron
- `r` = radius of orbit
- `n` = principal quantum number (n = 1, 2, 3, ...)
- `h` = Planck's constant = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s

**Postulate 3: Emission and Absorption of Energy (Quantum Jumps)**

*"When an electron jumps from a higher energy orbit (nᵢ) to a lower energy orbit (nf), it emits a photon. When it absorbs a photon, it jumps from a lower to a higher orbit."*

```
Energy of photon = Eᵢ − Ef = hν
```

```
         hc
hν =  ————————  =  Eᵢ − Ef
          λ
```

```
(Higher orbit)  nᵢ   →  nf  (Lower orbit)
                    ↘
                  hν (photon emitted)
```

### 6.3 Derivation of Bohr's Radius ⭐⭐ (5 Mark Derivation)

**Step 1: Centripetal Force = Electrostatic Force**

For an electron in circular orbit of radius r around the nucleus (charge Ze for hydrogen, Z=1):

```
    mv²      1    Ze²
  ——————  = ————  ———
     r      4πε₀   r²
```

```
         Ze²
mv² = ————————           ... (1)
       4πε₀r
```

**Step 2: Apply Bohr's Quantization Condition**

```
        nh
mvr = ——————
       2π

        nh
v = ——————————            ... (2)
      2πmr
```

**Step 3: Solve for radius r**

From (1): v² = Ze²/4πε₀mr

From (2): v² = n²h²/4π²m²r²

Equating:
```
Ze²/4πε₀mr = n²h²/4π²m²r²
```

Solving for r:
```
         n²h²ε₀
r = ——————————————
        πmZe²
```

For hydrogen (Z = 1), this becomes:

```
         n²h²ε₀
rₙ = ——————————————
          πme²
```

**For n = 1 (first orbit / ground state):**

```
       h²ε₀
a₀ = ————————  =  0.529 Å  =  0.529 × 10⁻¹⁰ m
       πme²
```

**This is called the Bohr radius (a₀ = 0.529 Å)**

**General formula:**

```
rₙ = n² × a₀ = n² × 0.529 Å
```

So:
- r₁ = 0.529 Å (n=1)
- r₂ = 4 × 0.529 = 2.116 Å (n=2)
- r₃ = 9 × 0.529 = 4.761 Å (n=3)

**Key result: rₙ ∝ n²** (radius increases as square of orbit number)

### 6.4 Derivation of Velocity of Electron in nth Orbit ⭐

From Bohr's quantization: mvr = nh/2π

Substituting r = n²a₀:

```
        e²        1
vₙ = ————————  × ——
       2ε₀h      n
```

Or simply:
```
        v₁
vₙ = ——————
         n
```

Where v₁ = 2.18 × 10⁶ m/s (speed in first orbit)

**Key result: vₙ ∝ 1/n** (speed decreases as orbit number increases)

## 7. Energy of Electron in Bohr's Orbit

### 7.1 Total Energy Derivation ⭐⭐ (5 Mark Derivation — Board Exam)

**Kinetic Energy (KE) of electron:**

From centripetal force = electrostatic force:
```
mv² = Ze²/4πε₀r

         Ze²
KE = ½mv² = ————————
              8πε₀r
```

**Potential Energy (PE) of electron:**

The PE of electron in the field of nucleus (charge +Ze) at distance r:
```
         −Ze²
PE = ————————————
          4πε₀r
```
(Negative because the force is attractive)

**Total Energy (E = KE + PE):**

```
E = KE + PE

    Ze²        Ze²
= ———————— − ————————
   8πε₀r      4πε₀r

      Ze²
= − ————————
     8πε₀r
```

Substituting r = n²a₀ (for hydrogen Z=1):

```
         me⁴         13.6
Eₙ = − ————————— = − ————— eV
        8ε₀²h²n²       n²
```

**This is the most important formula of this chapter.**

### 7.2 Energy Values for Hydrogen ⭐⭐

```
         −13.6
Eₙ = ————————— eV
           n²
```

| n (orbit) | Eₙ (eV) | State |
|---|---|---|
| n = 1 | **−13.6 eV** | Ground state |
| n = 2 | **−3.4 eV** | First excited state |
| n = 3 | **−1.51 eV** | Second excited state |
| n = 4 | **−0.85 eV** | Third excited state |
| n = ∞ | **0 eV** | Ionized state |

> 📝 **Important Points:**
> - Energy is **negative** because the electron is bound to the nucleus (bounded system).
> - As n increases, energy increases (becomes less negative → closer to zero).
> - The electron in n=1 is most tightly bound (lowest energy).
> - **Ionization energy** = energy needed to remove electron from ground state = +13.6 eV

### 7.3 Ionization Energy and Ionization Potential ⭐

**Ionization Energy:** Energy required to remove an electron completely from the atom (n=1 to n=∞).

```
Ionization Energy = E∞ − E₁ = 0 − (−13.6) = 13.6 eV
```

**Ionization Potential:** The potential difference through which an electron must be accelerated to gain ionization energy.

```
Ionization Potential = 13.6 V
```

### 7.4 Energy Level Diagram ⭐⭐

```
  n=∞  ————————————  E = 0 eV         (ionized)
  n=5  ————————————  E = −0.54 eV
  n=4  ————————————  E = −0.85 eV
  n=3  ————————————  E = −1.51 eV
  n=2  ————————————  E = −3.4 eV

  n=1  ════════════  E = −13.6 eV      (ground state)
```

Arrows pointing DOWN = emission (photon emitted)
Arrows pointing UP = absorption (photon absorbed)

### 7.5 Frequency and Wavelength of Emitted Photon ⭐⭐

When electron jumps from n₂ (higher) to n₁ (lower):

```
hν = Eₙ₂ − Eₙ₁

      13.6    13.6
hν = ——————  − ——————
       n₁²      n₂²

       1    13.6  ⎛ 1      1  ⎞
ν = ——————× ————— ⎜—————− —————⎟
       h      1   ⎝n₁²   n₂² ⎠
```

Using c = νλ:

```
1       ⎛ 1      1  ⎞
—— = R_H⎜—————− —————⎟
λ       ⎝n₁²   n₂² ⎠
```

Where **R_H = me⁴/8ε₀²h³c = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹** is the Rydberg constant.

This is the **Rydberg formula** — and it perfectly matches the experimentally observed spectrum of hydrogen!

## 8. Hydrogen Spectrum and Spectral Series

### 8.1 What are Spectral Series? ⭐⭐⭐ (Board Favourite — 3 Marks)

When electrons in hydrogen atoms make transitions between different energy levels, they emit photons of specific wavelengths. These form **series** of spectral lines.

Each series is named after its discoverer and corresponds to a specific final orbit (n₁).

### 8.2 All Spectral Series of Hydrogen ⭐⭐⭐

**General formula:**
```
1     ⎛  1       1  ⎞
—— = R⎜————— − ————— ⎟
λ     ⎝  n₁²    n₂² ⎠
```
where n₂ > n₁

---

#### Series 1: Lyman Series (UV Region)

- **Final orbit:** n₁ = 1
- **Initial orbits:** n₂ = 2, 3, 4, 5, ...
- **Region:** **Ultraviolet (UV)**
- **Formula:**
```
1/λ = R(1/1² − 1/n²),  n = 2, 3, 4, ...
```

---

#### Series 2: Balmer Series (Visible Region) ⭐ Most Important

- **Final orbit:** n₁ = 2
- **Initial orbits:** n₂ = 3, 4, 5, 6, ...
- **Region:** **Visible light** (some lines) + near UV
- **Formula:**
```
1/λ = R(1/2² − 1/n²),  n = 3, 4, 5, ...
```
- The first line (n=3→2) is called **Hα** (red, 656 nm)
- The second line (n=4→2) is called **Hβ** (blue-green, 486 nm)

---

#### Series 3: Paschen Series (IR Region)

- **Final orbit:** n₁ = 3
- **Initial orbits:** n₂ = 4, 5, 6, ...
- **Region:** **Infrared (IR)**
- **Formula:**
```
1/λ = R(1/3² − 1/n²),  n = 4, 5, 6, ...
```

---

#### Series 4: Brackett Series (Far IR)

- **Final orbit:** n₁ = 4
- **Initial orbits:** n₂ = 5, 6, 7, ...
- **Region:** **Far Infrared**
- **Formula:**
```
1/λ = R(1/4² − 1/n²),  n = 5, 6, 7, ...
```

---

#### Series 5: Pfund Series (Far IR)

- **Final orbit:** n₁ = 5
- **Initial orbits:** n₂ = 6, 7, 8, ...
- **Region:** **Far Infrared**
- **Formula:**
```
1/λ = R(1/5² − 1/n²),  n = 6, 7, 8, ...
```

---

### 8.3 Summary Table of Spectral Series ⭐⭐⭐

| Series | n₁ | n₂ | Region | Discovered By |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lyman** | 1 | 2, 3, 4, ... | Ultraviolet | Theodore Lyman |
| **Balmer** | 2 | 3, 4, 5, ... | Visible/near UV | Johann Balmer |
| **Paschen** | 3 | 4, 5, 6, ... | Infrared | Friedrich Paschen |
| **Brackett** | 4 | 5, 6, 7, ... | Far Infrared | Frederick Brackett |
| **Pfund** | 5 | 6, 7, 8, ... | Far Infrared | August Pfund |

> 📝 **Memory Trick for series order:** **L**ooks **B**eautiful, **P**retty **B**right **P**icture
> → **L**yman, **B**almer, **P**aschen, **B**rackett, **P**fund

> 📝 **Board Exam Tip:** Almost every year, there is a question: "Which series of hydrogen spectrum lies in the visible region?" → **Balmer Series**

### 8.4 Series Limit (Shortest Wavelength / Highest Frequency)

The **series limit** is the shortest wavelength line of any series — when n₂ → ∞:

```
1/λ_limit = R/n₁²
```

| Series | Series Limit (λ_min) |
|---|---|
| Lyman | λ = 1/R = 91.2 nm |
| Balmer | λ = 4/R = 364.6 nm |
| Paschen | λ = 9/R = 820.4 nm |

## 9. De Broglie's Explanation of Bohr's Postulate

### 9.1 Background — De Broglie Waves ⭐

In 1924, **Louis de Broglie** proposed that matter (like electrons) has **wave nature**.

The wavelength associated with a particle of momentum p:
```
         h
λ = ——————————
        mv
```

### 9.2 Explanation of Bohr's Second Postulate ⭐⭐

Bohr's second postulate states: mvr = nh/2π (angular momentum is quantized).

**De Broglie's explanation:**
- For an electron in a circular orbit, it behaves as a **standing wave**.
- A standing wave can only exist if the **circumference of the orbit is an integer multiple of the wavelength**:

```
2πr = nλ
```

Where n = 1, 2, 3, ...

Substituting de Broglie wavelength λ = h/mv:

```
2πr = n(h/mv)

mvr = nh/2π
```

**This is exactly Bohr's second postulate!**

De Broglie's wave theory thus **provides a physical basis** for Bohr's quantization condition — it's not just an arbitrary rule, it arises from the wave nature of electrons.

```
For n=3 orbit:        For n=4 orbit:
3 complete waves      4 complete waves
fit around orbit      fit around orbit

    ~~~                   ~~~~
  ~     ~               ~      ~
 ~       ~             ~        ~
  ~  +  ~               ~  +   ~
   ~   ~                 ~     ~
    ~~~                   ~~~~
```

> 📝 **Board Exam:** This topic is asked as "How does de Broglie's hypothesis explain Bohr's second postulate?" — 2 to 3 marks.

## 10. Limitations of Bohr's Model

### 10.1 Limitations ⭐ (Board Exam — 2 Marks)

1. **Works only for hydrogen-like atoms:** Bohr's model is valid only for single-electron atoms like H, He⁺, Li²⁺. It fails for multi-electron atoms.

2. **Cannot explain fine structure:** Spectral lines of hydrogen, when observed with high-resolution instruments, split into multiple closely-spaced lines (fine structure). Bohr's model cannot explain this.

3. **Cannot explain Zeeman effect:** When a magnetic field is applied to the atom, spectral lines split. Bohr's model fails to explain this (Zeeman effect).

4. **Cannot explain Stark effect:** Splitting of spectral lines in an electric field — Bohr's model cannot account for it.

5. **No explanation for relative intensities:** Bohr's model cannot predict why some spectral lines are brighter (more intense) than others.

6. **No wave nature of electron:** Bohr's model treats the electron as a particle, ignoring its wave nature (which de Broglie later proposed).

7. **Violates Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle:** Bohr assumed that both the position and momentum of an electron in an orbit can be precisely known simultaneously, which contradicts Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle.

**Q12. Write Bohr's three postulates.**

**Ans:**

2. Angular momentum of electron in stationary orbit: mvr = nh/2π
3. When electron jumps from higher to lower orbit, it emits a photon of energy hν = Eᵢ − Ef

**Q15. Explain how de Broglie's hypothesis justifies Bohr's second postulate.**

**Ans:**
De Broglie proposed that electron has wave nature with λ = h/mv.
For a stable orbit, the electron must form a standing wave:
```
2πr = nλ = n(h/mv)
∴ mvr = nh/2π
```
This is exactly Bohr's second postulate. Hence de Broglie's wave theory gives physical justification to Bohr's quantization condition.

**Q16. Describe Geiger-Marsden's alpha particle scattering experiment. What are its conclusions?**

**Q17. What is the formula for distance of closest approach? Derive it.**

**Ans:**

```
KE = PE

½mv² = (1/4πε₀) × 2Ze²/r₀

       1     2Ze²       2kZe²
r₀ = ————  ———————  = ——————————
     4πε₀   ½mv²          E_k
```

This gives the nuclear size ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ m.

### 📝 Numerical Problems (Board Pattern)

**Num 1:** Calculate the radius of the third Bohr orbit in hydrogen atom.

**Solution:**
```
rₙ = n² × 0.529 Å

r₃ = 3² × 0.529 = 9 × 0.529 = 4.761 Å ≈ 4.76 Å
```
**Answer: r₃ = 4.76 Å = 4.76 × 10⁻¹⁰ m**

---

**Num 2:** Find the energy of electron in the second excited state of hydrogen.

**Solution:**
```
Second excited state means n = 3 (ground = n=1, first excited = n=2, second excited = n=3)

E₃ = −13.6/3² = −13.6/9 = −1.51 eV
```
**Answer: E₃ = −1.51 eV**

---

**Num 3:** Calculate the wavelength of Hα line (first Balmer line, n=3→n=2) in hydrogen spectrum. (R = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹)

**Solution:**
```
Balmer series: 1/λ = R(1/2² − 1/3²) = R(1/4 − 1/9)

= 1.097×10⁷ × (9−4)/36

= 1.097×10⁷ × 5/36

= 1.524×10⁶ m⁻¹

λ = 1/(1.524×10⁶) = 6.56×10⁻⁷ m = 656 nm
```
**Answer: λ = 656 nm (red colour — Hα line)**

---

**Num 4:** An electron in hydrogen atom jumps from n=4 to n=2. Find the frequency of the emitted photon. (h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)

**Solution:**
```
E₄ = −13.6/16 = −0.85 eV
E₂ = −13.6/4 = −3.4 eV

Energy emitted: ΔE = E₄ − E₂ = −0.85 − (−3.4) = 2.55 eV
                   = 2.55 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 4.08×10⁻¹⁹ J

hν = ΔE
ν = ΔE/h = 4.08×10⁻¹⁹ / 6.626×10⁻³⁴
  = 6.16×10¹⁴ Hz
```
**Answer: ν ≈ 6.16 × 10¹⁴ Hz**

---

**Num 5:** What is the maximum number of spectral lines emitted when hydrogen atom is excited to n=4 state?

**Solution:**
```
Number of spectral lines = n(n−1)/2
For n = 4: lines = 4×3/2 = 6
```

The possible transitions:
4→3, 4→2, 4→1, 3→2, 3→1, 2→1 = **6 lines**

**Answer: 6 spectral lines**

---

**Num 6:** Find the distance of closest approach when an alpha particle of KE = 5 MeV is aimed at a gold nucleus (Z = 79).

**Solution:**
```
r₀ = 2kZe²/E_k

k = 9×10⁹ Nm²C⁻²
Z = 79, e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C
E_k = 5 MeV = 5×10⁶ × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 8×10⁻¹³ J

r₀ = (2 × 9×10⁹ × 79 × (1.6×10⁻¹⁹)²) / (8×10⁻¹³)

   = (2 × 9×10⁹ × 79 × 2.56×10⁻³⁸) / (8×10⁻¹³)

   = (3.638×10⁻²⁶) / (8×10⁻¹³)

   = 4.55×10⁻¹⁴ m ≈ 45.5 fm
```
**Answer: r₀ ≈ 4.55 × 10⁻¹⁴ m**

---

**Num 7:** Calculate the speed of electron in the first Bohr orbit of hydrogen. (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s, m = 9.1×10⁻³¹ kg, a₀ = 0.529×10⁻¹⁰ m)

**Solution:**
```
mvr = nh/2π  (n=1)

v₁ = h/(2πma₀)
   = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (2π × 9.1×10⁻³¹ × 0.529×10⁻¹⁰)
   = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (3.02×10⁻⁴⁰)
   = 2.19×10⁶ m/s
```
**Answer: v₁ ≈ 2.19 × 10⁶ m/s** (about 1% of speed of light)

### 💡 Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Don't say "first excited state = n=1"** — ground state is n=1, first excited state is n=2, second excited state is n=3, and so on.

2. **Energy formula: Eₙ = −13.6/n²** — note the NEGATIVE sign. Energy is always negative for bound electrons.

3. **Direction of transition for emission vs absorption:** Emission = electron drops DOWN (higher n to lower n). Absorption = electron jumps UP (lower n to higher n).

4. **Balmer series is visible, not Lyman.** Lyman is UV. Many students confuse these.

5. **Impact parameter b and scattering angle θ are inversely related** — smaller b = larger θ, not the other way.

6. **De Broglie explains Bohr's SECOND postulate** (quantization of angular momentum), NOT the first or third.

7. **Ionization energy = 13.6 eV (positive)**, not −13.6 eV. You need to supply this energy to remove the electron.

8. **Number of spectral lines = n(n−1)/2**, not just n or n−1.

9. **Bohr's model works ONLY for hydrogen and hydrogen-like ions** (He⁺, Li²⁺, etc.) — not for multi-electron atoms.

10. **In the energy formula for non-hydrogen atoms:** Eₙ = −13.6 Z²/n² eV, where Z is the atomic number. Don't forget the Z² factor for problems involving He⁺ etc.
